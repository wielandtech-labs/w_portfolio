## Problem

Bars and cafés have a captive audience in their restrooms and no easy way to make money from it.
Advertisers want local reach without the creepiness of tracked ads. Making a two-sided network
work for a handful of venues means being fair about the revenue split, and private about the
people scanning.

## Approach

- **Two faces, one app.** A straight-laced agency site for venues and advertisers, and a playful
  guerrilla site for patrons. The request's host picks the face, and short codes work on both.
- **Fair split.** Revenue for a period is shared in proportion to each venue's de-duplicated
  traffic. A visitor's identity is a daily HMAC of request attributes plus a one-day cookie, so
  repeat scans don't inflate a share and no raw IP is ever stored.
- **Operations built in.** Patrons can rate a restroom or report a problem. Staff get a push
  alert (deduplicated per sticker and issue), and notifications can never break the request
  that triggered them.

## Hard parts

- **A leaderboard that never shames.** The "cleanest restroom" ranking lists only venues that
  opted in and have enough ratings, ranked by a Bayesian average. The network-wide mean includes
  unlisted venues, so opting in can't shift the bar.
- **Legal text that can't silently drift.** Venue agreements and advertiser terms are versioned
  files pinned by hash. Changing the text means a new version, not an edit.
- **Real-browser-only bugs.** A strict referrer policy made browsers send `Origin: null` on the
  page's own form posts, which failed CSRF checks. The test client never sends an Origin header,
  so a regression test now guards it.

## Outcome

- Live, with the venue onboarding, sticker printing, scan, feedback and payout flows in place.
- Every printed code is permanent, and the system is built around that constraint.
